The reconciliation problem

Your deposit never matches your sales total.

And most tools paper over the difference instead of accounting for it. That's fine until an accountant, a lender, or a tax authority asks you to prove the numbers.

// THE GAP

Payouts arrive net, not gross

Fees, refunds, and adjustments come out before the money lands. Booked as a single deposit, they quietly shrink your revenue and hide what really happened.

// THE SYNC

Sync tools push summaries, not a ledger

Most connectors send a rolled-up figure into your accounting software and hope it reconciles. When it doesn't, there's no underlying record to trace back to.

// THE TAX

Sales tax gets booked as income

Tax you collect isn't revenue — it's money you owe. Tax a marketplace remits for you isn't yours at all. Conflate them and every downstream number is off.

What the engine does

A ledger built to be the source of truth.

Not a bridge into someone else's software — the accounting record itself, structured the way an accountant would keep it.

01 / LEDGER

A real ledger, not a sync tool

Every payout posts as balanced double-entry against a full chart of accounts. Your P&L, balance sheet, and trial balance always tie out — because they're generated from the ledger, not reconstructed after the fact.

Source of truth

02 / PAYOUTS

Payouts decomposed, not netted

Processing fees, refunds, and adjustments are separated out and posted to their own accounts instead of vanishing into a net deposit — then reconciled against the actual bank transaction, with any mismatch flagged rather than absorbed.

Every line explained

03 / TAX

Sales tax as a liability

Tax collected on Shopify is tracked as a liability by state. Tax a marketplace facilitator remits for you is netted correctly to zero — a distinction most bookkeeping setups get wrong, and one that matters at filing time.

Tracked by state
